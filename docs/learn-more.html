Two pods. One line of sight.

Everything in the frame.

How the picture gets to your eye

A screen the size of a stamp.

A 0.96 inch OLED sits in the right pod. White pixels on black. Black is what makes this work: an unlit pixel is simply transparent later on.

Fold one

Turn the corner.

A front-surface mirror at 45 degrees sends the light along the brow, toward your nose, instead of out the front of the pod.

The lens

Push the image out to arm's length.

Your eye cannot focus on something two centimetres away. A 50 mm lens makes the screen appear about 25 cm in front of you.

Fold two

Down, over the eye.

Inside the hood a second mirror turns the beam straight down, right above your right eye.

The half-mirror

Half of the world, half of the screen.

A 50/50 beam splitter tilted at 45 degrees. Light from the room passes through. Light from the screen bounces into your eye. Both at once, on the same line of sight.

Try it

This is the whole screen.

128 by 64 pixels, white on black, drawn exactly as the glasses will draw it. Ask something. The real thing answers in your ear as well.
